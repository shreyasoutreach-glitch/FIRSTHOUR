# PRIMHORA Market Evidence

Research date: 2026-09-30

## 1. Evidence standard

This document separates:
- VERIFIED FACT: directly supported by a primary or high-quality source.
- ATTRIBUTED ANALYSIS: interpretation reported by a source.
- FOUNDER ASSESSMENT: a sales/segment judgement, not a published statistic.
- UNVERIFIED: reliable public data was not found.

URLs are retained so every material number can be independently checked.

## 2. India fraud evidence

### 2025 NCRP / I4C data: do not collapse different series

The Ministry of Home Affairs Rajya Sabha answer dated 11 Feb 2026 states that CFCFRMS had saved more than Rs 8,189 crore across more than 23.61 lakh complaints through 31 Dec 2025. It does NOT, in that answer, provide the 2025 Rs 22,495 crore figure or an exact 24.02 lakh financial-fraud complaint count.

Source: MHA Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question 1349, 11 Feb 2026:
https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2026-pdfs/RS11022026/1349.pdf
Confidence: HIGH.

A separate MHA Rajya Sabha answer, Question 1338, dated 11 Feb 2026, states that by 31 Dec 2025 the I4C Suspect Registry had received more than 21.65 lakh suspect-identifier records from banks and shared 26.48 lakh Layer-1 mule accounts with participating entities. Transactions worth Rs 9,055.27 crore were declined through the registry.

Source: MHA Rajya Sabha Question 1338, 11 Feb 2026:
https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2026-pdfs/RS11022026/1338.pdf
Confidence: HIGH.

PIB later reported, on 21 Aug 2026, that India recorded approximately Rs 22,495 crore in cyber-fraud losses and 28.15 lakh complaints in 2025. The release says the figures came from a presentation at a PIB media workshop and describes them as reported figures. It is not the same document as the February parliamentary statistical annexure.

Source: PIB Kohima, Release ID 2301973, 21 Aug 2026:
https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2301973
Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.

A secondary IMPRI analysis reports NCRP financial-fraud complaints rising from 2.62 lakh in 2021 to 24.02 lakh in 2025. Treat this as a secondary reconstruction, not interchangeable with the broader 28.15 lakh 2025 complaint figure above.

Source: IMPRI, 2026:
https://impriinsights.in/indian-cyber-crime-coordination-centre-i4c-strengthening-indias-response-to-cyber-fraud/
Confidence: MEDIUM.

### Recovery evidence

The MHA February 2026 answer reports Rs 8,189 crore saved through CFCFRMS by 31 Dec 2025, across more than 23.61 lakh complaints. This is an intervention/saving measure, not proof that victims ultimately recovered that money.

Source: MHA, Question 1349, 11 Feb 2026:
https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2026-pdfs/RS11022026/1349.pdf
Confidence: HIGH.

ACFE's 2026 global occupational-fraud research provides a different population. It covers 2,402 real occupational-fraud cases from 143 countries and reports a median loss of $104,000 per case. CFEs estimate organizations lose 5% of revenue to fraud annually.

Source: ACFE Occupational Fraud 2026:
https://www.acfe.com/fraud-resources/report-to-the-nations
https://www.acfe.com/about-the-acfe/newsroom-for-media/press-releases/press-release-detail?s=report-to-the-
Publication: May 2026.
Confidence: HIGH for the ACFE sample, LOW for using it as an India-wide loss estimate.

ACFE also reports that only 15% of organizations in its studied cases recovered all losses, while 56% recovered nothing. This is global occupational fraud evidence, not an Indian cyber-fraud recovery rate.

Source: ACFE, September 2026:
https://www.acfe.com/acfe-insights-blog/blog-detail?s=hidden-costs-of-fraud-beyond-financial-loss
Confidence: HIGH for the ACFE population; LOW for direct India extrapolation.

### RBI bank-fraud evidence

RBI Annual Report 2025-26 reports 10,114 bank fraud cases involving Rs 48,021 crore in FY2025-26, versus 23,722 cases involving Rs 32,803 crore in FY2024-25. Advances accounted for approximately 85% of the FY2025-26 amount.

Primary source:
https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/AnnualReport/PDFs/0AR29052026F5B979AF274E445ABB1593EB226906335.PDF
Publication: 29 May 2026.
Confidence: HIGH.

The headline fall in card/internet/digital-payment fraud cases to 293 should NOT be interpreted as a simple collapse in digital fraud. The RBI data also contains major reclassification/reporting effects: 314 cases involving Rs 30,199 crore in FY2025-26 related to previous financial years and were reported afresh after re-examination. The composition of reported fraud shifted strongly toward advances.

Supporting reporting of RBI data:
https://indianexpress.com/article/business/bank-frauds-amount-rises-46-pc-to-rs-48000-crore-in-fy26-rbi-annual-report-10713875/
https://www.business-standard.com/industry/banking/bank-fraud-cases-halve-but-value-climbs-to-48-000-crore-rbi-data-126052900631_1.html
Confidence: HIGH.

## 3. B2B / vendor-payment / BEC evidence

India-specific public data that cleanly measures vendor bank-detail-change fraud, B2B payout fraud, or internal payout misuse by company size is scarce.

That absence is itself important: PRIMHORA must not manufacture an India TAM from consumer-fraud numbers.

CERT-In's 7 Aug 2026 advisory on emerging Microsoft 365 threats explicitly describes Business Email Compromise, compromised trusted suppliers, fraudulent internal/external communications, and financial fraud. It recommends preserving logs/evidence and initiating incident response when compromise is suspected.

Source: CERT-In Advisory CIAD-2026-0037, 7 Aug 2026:
https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?VLCODE=CIAD-2026-0037&pageid=PUBVLNOTES02
Confidence: HIGH.

The FBI IC3 describes BEC as a scam targeting businesses and individuals performing transfers of funds and explicitly notes fraudulent payment redirection and changes to bank-account destinations. Its 2025 brochure reports $3 billion in BEC losses in 2025 in its U.S. complaint dataset.

Sources:
https://www.ic3.gov/CrimeInfo/BEC
https://www.ic3.gov/Outreach/Brochures/ic3-brochure.pdf
Publication: 2025 data, brochure published 2026.
Confidence: HIGH for IC3's U.S./reported population; LOW for direct India sizing.

The FBI's 2024 BEC PSA reported more than $55.49 billion in identified global exposed BEC losses between Oct 2013 and Dec 2023. This is a global IC3-derived dataset, not an India estimate.

Source:
https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240911
Publication: 11 Sep 2024.
Confidence: HIGH for the IC3 dataset.

Sources:

**investigation and evidence reconstruction after a financial incident or suspected payment diversion**, especially where the source data is fragmented across payment exports, bank statements, emails, invoices and human attestations.

## 8. Target segment hypothesis

### Primary commercial hypothesis: mid-market finance/AP/operations teams

Why:
- They control real payment data.
- CSV exports are a credible initial data-access path.
- The workflow can be sold without moving money.
- A single incident can justify investigation tooling without requiring a bank partnership.
- The target is large enough to pay but more reachable than banks or government.
- The category is competitive, so PRIMHORA must sell investigation/evidence reconstruction rather than generic prevention.

### Secondary / channel hypothesis: CA and forensic-accounting firms

Why:
- ICAI provides a large professional distribution network.
- 5,374 firms and 6,616 members are currently tagged for forensic accounting/investigation in the ICAI directory.
- Forensic practitioners naturally receive evidence packages from clients.
- PRIMHORA's read-only evidence reconstruction and packet generation aligns with an investigation workflow.
- A firm could become both a user and a distribution channel into client organizations.

## 9. What would prove this hypothesis wrong?

The top segment hypothesis should be considered falsified or materially weakened if customer discovery shows any of the following:

1. Mid-market finance teams do not experience enough payment/reconciliation incidents to justify a recurring workflow.
2. Teams already have sufficiently good investigation tooling inside their ERP/AP/fraud stack and will not adopt a separate read-only evidence workspace.
3. Customers will not upload transaction exports or evidence because of privacy/security concerns.
4. The value of faster investigation is not high enough to support paid software.
5. CA/forensic firms prefer spreadsheets/document repositories and will not change their investigation workflow.
6. PRIMHORA cannot achieve materially better evidence traceability or investigation speed than existing enterprise suites.

These are hypotheses. No public dataset can settle them. Customer interviews and observed usage must do that.

## 10. Key market conclusion

The evidence supports a real problem, but it does NOT yet prove a large Indian software market for PRIMHORA.

The strongest verified evidence is:
- financial fraud is occurring at national scale;
- I4C is already operating large-scale suspect/mule-account and financial-fraud coordination infrastructure;
- occupational fraud creates material organizational losses and slow detection;
- recovery is frequently incomplete;
- Indian forensic-accounting infrastructure is substantial;
- BEC and vendor/payment diversion are recognized organizational threats;
- existing payment-fraud vendors prove companies will buy adjacent controls.

The unverified part is the commercial leap:
**how many Indian mid-market companies will pay specifically for a read-only financial-incident investigation/evidence platform, at what price, and how often.**

That must be validated through customer discovery, not guessed from consumer-fraud totals.
